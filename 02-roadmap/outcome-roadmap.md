# Prioritization & Roadmapping, Module 2 Lab

## Prioritize your Rocks and your Hard Nos
- **Rock #1:** Simplify the daily log from 14 required fields to 4, removing the biggest friction in capturing field updates and driving repeat field usage.
- **Rock #2:** Build an offline-first mobile experience so Meridian remains usable at the point of work even on poorly connected job sites.
- **Rock #3:** Add photo markup and annotation so field teams can document progress and issues in Meridian as naturally as using their phone camera.
- **❌ Hard No #1:** #10: Executive reporting dashboard with custom KPIs  
Deprioritize the executive reporting dashboard despite two renewals being at risk. It deepens the administrative experience but does not close the field-adoption gap this strategy is designed to solve.
- **❌ Hard No #2:** #5: Automated compliance checklist generator  
Deprioritize the automated compliance checklist generator despite enterprise demand from three clients. It adds more back-office depth before Meridian has solved frontline adoption.
- **❌ Hard No #3:** #12: Time-tracking module  
Deprioritize time tracking despite active contract negotiations. It may support individual deals, but it does not strengthen Meridian’s chosen advantage of turning field documentation into the enterprise record.

## Show and swap
- **Do the Rock selections feel traceable to a clear strategy, or do they read like a feature list?:** The Rocks trace directly to the strategy: simplify capture removes friction, offline-first makes Meridian available at the point of work, and photo markup competes directly with the camera workflow field teams use today. Together they target adoption and the shift of field updates into Meridian rather than adding unrelated product breadth.
- **Pick one Hard No and make the case for why it should actually be a Rock.:** The executive reporting dashboard has the strongest case to become a Rock because two renewals are already at risk, creating immediate revenue pressure. I would still keep it out: protecting those renewals with more executive functionality treats a near-term symptom while leaving the structural field-adoption problem unresolved. If field usage remains weak, Meridian’s long-term differentiation continues to erode.

## Review and refine
- **Does every Now item read as a strategic bet, or does it sound like a feature description?:** Yes. Each Now item describes the behavioral or workflow change we want to create; the backlog feature is the mechanism, not the outcome.
- **Can you trace every Now item back to one of your OKRs?:** Yes. Faster and offline field capture drive weekly adoption and the share of updates created in Meridian, while photo-based workflows also increase the share of field information entering the enterprise record without manual re-entry.
- **If someone who had never seen your strategy read the Now column, would they know what problem you are solving this quarter?:** - Yes. The Now column makes clear that this quarter is focused on replacing informal field documentation with a low-friction, reliable Meridian workflow at the point of work.
