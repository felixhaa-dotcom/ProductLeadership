# Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test, Module 1 Lab

## Write your one-page strategy
- **Winning aspiration: define winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics. What does success look like for the specific person you serve?:** Meridian becomes the tool field teams choose instead of their camera roll and group chat, because updating the project record is as easy as documenting the work itself.
- **Where to play: segment, geography, and use case, explicitly. Which customers, markets, and surfaces will you focus on?:** Superintendents and foremen on $50M+ construction projects in the US, in the moments they would otherwise use their phone camera or group chat to document site progress, flag issues, or coordinate next actions.
- **How to win: your differentiator. What can you do that your specific competitors cannot easily replicate?:** Give field teams the speed of a camera and group chat while automatically turning their updates into the trusted enterprise record their companies already run on — without duplicate entry.
- **Capabilities required: what you must be world-class at. What will you build, buy, or partner for?:** Build: a field-first mobile experience that makes capturing progress, issues, and decisions as fast as using a phone camera or group chat.
Build: automatic structuring and routing of lightweight field inputs into Meridian’s existing project workflows without duplicate entry.
Partner where needed: device, communication, and enterprise integrations that accelerate adoption without becoming the source of differentiation.
- **Management systems: the metrics and rituals that keep your choices alive quarter to quarter.:** Weekly field adoption review: active field users, repeat usage, and share of site updates originating in Meridian.
Monthly field-to-enterprise quality review: completeness, structure, and downstream usage of field-created information.
Quarterly customer outcome review: duplicate-entry reduction, issue-resolution speed, and field adoption across participating projects.

## Name your one hard no
- **Your one hard no:** We will not use this investment cycle to deepen enterprise analytics, ERP integrations, or top-10 contractor features, because adding more administrative depth does not solve the field-adoption gap and would pull resources away from making Meridian indispensable on the job site.

## Write your three OKRs
- **Objective:** Make Meridian the default way superintendents and foremen document and communicate what happens on site.
- **KR1:** KR1: ≥60% of target superintendents and foremen are weekly active users on participating projects within two quarters of launch.
- **KR2:** KR2: ≥50% of field progress and issue updates on participating projects are captured directly in Meridian rather than external photo/chat workflows within two quarters of launch.
- **KR3:** KR3: ≥80% of field-created updates enter the enterprise project record without manual re-entry within two quarters of launch.

## AI pressure-test
- **Which challenge from the AI is most valid, and why?:** The strategy assumes Meridian’s existing enterprise position creates a durable advantage in the field, but a mobile-first competitor could potentially offer a better field experience and integrate back into enterprise systems. Why will field users choose Meridian rather than the best standalone field tool?
- **What would you change based on the pushback, and what would you defend?:** The challenge is valid because enterprise integration alone is not a sufficient moat. I would sharpen the strategy by making zero-duplicate-entry and seamless field-to-enterprise workflow the core differentiator, rather than integration itself. I would defend the decision to prioritize the field over deeper enterprise features: Meridian already has enterprise adoption, while the unresolved strategic gap is frontline usage.
