The case

Five deliverables, one strategy

Each lab committed one decision. Together they are the bet, the sequence, the team that owns it, the money, and what you learned building it.

Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Winning aspiration: Meridian becomes the tool field teams choose instead of their camera roll and group chat. Where to play: US superintendents and foremen on $50M+ projects, at the moments they document progress, flag issues, or coordinate next actions. How to win: give them the speed of a camera and group chat while automatically turning their updates into the trusted enterprise record their companies already run on — without duplicate entry.

The hard no

We will not invest this cycle in deeper enterprise analytics, ERP integrations, or top-10 contractor features. Winning frontline adoption comes first.

Objective and key results

Objective: Make Meridian the default way superintendents and foremen document and communicate what happens on site. KR1: ≥60% of target field users are weekly active within two quarters. KR2: ≥50% of field progress and issue updates are captured directly in Meridian. KR3: ≥80% of field-created updates enter the enterprise project record without manual re-entry.

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

Now — Foundations team: Make field updates fast enough to happen in the flow of work, make Meridian reliable on poorly connected job sites, and move photo-based field documentation into the enterprise record.
Next — Foundations team with Customer Success: Use targeted notifications to bring field teams back when project conditions change, then test whether proven adoption justifies a dedicated foreman mobile surface.
Later — Foundations + Core Platform: Extend field participation into subcontractor workflows and AI-assisted coordination only after the core field workflow has proven sustained adoption.

Trade-off memo

We sequenced field capture first because adoption fails at the point of work: reducing input friction, enabling offline use, and replacing the camera workflow directly attack that barrier. We pushed notifications and a standalone foreman app until the core workflow proves repeat usage. We cut executive reporting, compliance automation, and time tracking from this cycle despite commercial pressure because they add breadth without closing the field-adoption gap.

Module 3
Team charter
What we own

We own the field experience for superintendents and foremen from capturing progress, issues, and decisions on site through to their successful entry into Meridian’s enterprise project record. This includes mobile field workflows, offline use, photo-based documentation, daily-log simplification, and the adoption outcomes in our OKRs. Core enterprise reporting, ERP integrations, finance and compliance workflows remain outside our scope.

How we decide

The Foundations PM decides feature and scope calls within the agreed quarterly Rocks. Changes affecting the enterprise data model, shared platform behavior, or customer commitments require a joint decision with the relevant core-platform owner. Cross-team conflicts escalate within 24 hours to the relevant PMs and engineering leads; priority or resource escalations from Sales or executives are resolved by the Foundations Product Leader.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

Fund a two-quarter validation of the offline-first field experience before committing to a broader rollout. The bet is that reliable field access moves documentation from camera rolls and group chats into Meridian, increasing frontline adoption and making accounts more likely to retain or expand Meridian across projects. The load-bearing assumption is that higher field adoption creates measurable commercial value, not just more usage. During the pilot we will validate both sides of that chain: ≥60% weekly active field adoption and, as a model assumption, at least a 5 percentage-point higher renewal or expansion rate among high-adoption pilot accounts versus a comparable low-adoption cohort. Exact ARR and payback will be calculated from Meridian’s actual contract and renewal data before any scale investment is approved.

Kill criterion

If weekly active field adoption remains below 40% after two quarters, stop the initiative. If adoption clears 40% but high-adoption pilot accounts do not show at least a 5 percentage-point higher renewal or expansion rate than a comparable low-adoption cohort, do not fund broader rollout or the standalone foreman app. In either case, reallocate the remaining Foundations capacity to the core platform.

Module 6

What I learned

Where the strategy was hardest to write, what you will keep doing, and the moment your thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was holding the strategic no when commercially attractive requests competed with the field-adoption bet. The work became much clearer once I treated “not now” as part of the strategy rather than as a prioritization failure.

Key learnings

I will trace major roadmap items back to explicit outcomes, separate stakeholder urgency from strategic importance, and define the kill criterion before committing significant capacity.

Aha moment

The biggest shift was realizing that a business case is not complete when the upside looks attractive; it is complete when I have also defined what evidence would make me stop funding it.
