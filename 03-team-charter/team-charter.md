## Name the situation
- **Who they are (role, not name), what you have observed, and how long it has been happening.:** A Product Manager on my team has repeatedly accepted stakeholder scope changes during the sprint without aligning them with engineering or checking whether they still fit the agreed priorities. This has happened several times over the past six weeks and has caused rework and confusion about what the team is actually committed to.

## Make a diagnosis
- **Your diagnosis, plus one sentence on why. Is your frustration with their behavior, or with a decision you made?:** Clarity problem. I had not made the decision rights for in-quarter scope changes explicit enough, so the PM was operating in an area where ownership and escalation expectations were unclear. My frustration is therefore partly with a leadership gap I created, not just with their behavior.

## Write your opening line
- **One next action I will take in the next two weeks is:** Clarify and document the decision rights for scope changes, then align them with the PM and engineering lead before the next sprint planning session.
- **The first sentence of the conversation I need to have is:** I realized I have not been clear enough about who can change scope once a sprint has started, and that has put you in a difficult position when stakeholders come directly to you.

## AI role-play
- **After you step out: what did the role-play change about how you will open this conversation for real?:** The role-play made me open with my own lack of clarity rather than with the PM’s behavior. That makes the conversation less defensive while still allowing me to set a clear expectation that future scope changes need an explicit decision before the team acts on them.

## Refine and complete your charter
- **What We Own. What this team owns.:** We own the field experience for superintendents and foremen from capturing progress, issues, and decisions on site through to their successful entry into Meridian’s enterprise project record. This includes field mobile workflows, offline usage, photo-based documentation, daily-log simplification, and the adoption outcomes defined in our OKRs.
- **What is out of scope.:** Core enterprise reporting and analytics, ERP integrations, finance and compliance workflows, and features built primarily for executive or back-office users remain owned by the respective core-platform teams.
- **Cross-boundary decisions that need a joint call.:** Any change that alters the enterprise data model, existing project-management workflows, or customer commitments requires a joint decision with the relevant core-platform or commercial owner before implementation.
- **How We Decide. Who decides feature and scope calls.:** The Meridian Foundations PM decides feature and scope calls within the agreed quarterly Rocks, as long as they stay within the team’s ownership boundaries and do not change enterprise commitments or shared platform behavior.
- **How cross-team conflicts escalate.:** Cross-team conflicts that cannot be resolved asynchronously are escalated within 24 hours to a joint decision call between the relevant PMs and engineering leads. Decisions are made against the agreed OKRs and strategic priorities, not stakeholder seniority.
- **Who resolves escalations from outside the team.:** The Product Leader for Meridian Foundations resolves escalations from sales, enterprise, or executive stakeholders when the request would change priorities, scope, or resource allocation for the initiative.

## Show and swap your team charter
- **Where does the charter leave room for interpretation that could cause a conflict?:** The main remaining ambiguity is what qualifies as a change to “shared platform behavior.” To avoid interpretation conflicts, we would define this explicitly as any change to the enterprise data model, permissions, core workflow logic, or contractual customer commitments.
